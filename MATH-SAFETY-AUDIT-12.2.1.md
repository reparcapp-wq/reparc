# RepArc 12.2.1 — calculation and first-use recommendation audit

## Conclusion and boundaries

This is a bounded software/research review, not proof that every possible input is correct, an independent coach sign-off or a prospective validation of individual recommendations. It audited the calculation paths listed below, added independently calculated expected values and adversarial fixtures, corrected confirmed defects and retained existing regression coverage. The new tests were written during this same development session; they are not independent external certification.

Same-exercise records from another day, slot, swap or program can inform a first session in a new program. A genuinely unseen exercise has no measured personal load baseline. Related-exercise familiarity does not justify converting another lift's kg, another person's records, sex, bodyweight or training age into an exact new-exercise working weight. The existing light calibration / own confirmed starting-weight path remains. Rep targets come from the selected program and return/calibration policy; they are not an exact prediction of the user's maximum reps.

## Confirmed corrections

| Path | Failure / misleading result | Correction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Prescribed-set completion | Extra sets of one exercise replaced missing sets of another in daily/period percentages, completion/adherence and workout-save classification. | One shared count of filled prescribed positions; extras still count as actual performed work. Save, reports and completed-day checks use the same rule. |
| External-load volume | Assistance counterweight and unloaded movements could inflate lifted-load totals. | Exclude those loading types and unidentified movements from this metric; weighted-bodyweight entries count added weight only. This remains a descriptive load × reps statistic, not mechanical work. |
| Assisted progression | High reported RIR could trigger a harder setting despite missing the lower rep target. A suggested assistance setting could equal/exceed known bodyweight. | Require the rep target on every next-session set; filter unusable assistance settings when bodyweight is known. |
| Mixed working weights | A median could become an unsupported fixed target for lighter back-off sets. A rep-miss decrease from the median could be heavier than the failed set. | No single numeric next-session target from varying external weights; a decrease is below the failed set itself. For assistance, use more assistance than the failed setting, not the unrelated last set. |
| Weigh-in averages | Duplicate days/revisions, superseded deletions and future entries could distort the dashboard average. One prior observation could imply a reliable weekly comparison. | Resolve current IDs/tombstones; one deterministic latest entry per date, exclude future/invalid entries, require at least three dates in BOTH windows for a percent comparison. Missing comparison is null, not zero. The displayed ending date identifies an older latest-record window. |
| Historical mass entry | Adding a past weigh-in silently replaced the current bodyweight used by exercise recommendations. | Past entries update history only; today's entry can update current mass, with unit conversion. This cannot reconstruct a current mass overwritten by an earlier app version—verify it in Setup if uncertain. |
| Protected trend claims | A developing exposure with progression disabled could be compared as a performance gain. | Protected current exposures are calibration; protected previous exposures cannot be a trend baseline. |
| Bodyweight unit boundary | 300 kg converted to 661.386… lb exceeded the old 660 lb reader limit, losing otherwise valid profile/session/weigh-in values during normalization. | Consistent physical upper bound and tolerance across readers; new-entry ranges derive from 25–300 kg and are inward-rounded for two-decimal input. Readers retain the older permissive lower bound for compatibility; implausible legacy mass is not used for a new history-based bodyweight recommendation. These are data-quality bounds, not clinical eligibility or safety limits. |

## Calculation inventory checked

- **Units and loading:** exact 1 lb = 0.45359237 kg; 500 round trips, per-side volume scaling, sparse equipment ladders, downward external-load rounding and upward assistance rounding.
- **History starting guidance:** paired first-set load/reps/effort; canonical matching, no cross-exercise weights; 14/28/56/180-day boundaries, one strictest factor, no repeated discount of recent successfully tolerated protected work. Source age/freshness and exact factors remain product policy, not measured strength loss.
- **Bodyweight and assistance:** old/current mass conversion; estimated resistance old mass minus assistance or plus added load; recorded mass required; no assistance >= current mass when producing a numeric history estimate; no fabricated weight when bodyweight alone exceeds a reduced target. Machine mechanics are not modeled.
- **Within/next-workout adjustment:** rep miss, failure, missing effort, readiness, step limits, mixed weights, protected increases, reverse assistance direction. Actual RIR remains subjective. The 10% step bound is an engineering limit, not a validated safety cutoff.
- **SBS Phase 2:** all 21 weeks remain finite with deloads at 7/14/21; the entire piecewise TM-change map is checked. Existing detailed replay tests cover exact prescribed work, equipment rounding, unit changes, manual review boundaries, duplicates, deletions and adverse recovery. Published tables are not replaced by a new predictive formula.
- **Reports:** hand-calculated reps, known-effort mean/coverage, prescribed-position completion, duration coverage, direct/indirect muscle sets and physical versus scheduled catch-up dates. Per-side work counts once per muscle, twice in external-load volume where appropriate. Counts do not measure muscle growth or establish an optimal dose.
- **Bodyweight trends:** shared distinct-day semantics, deterministic ties, two-window sufficiency, percent denominator, no invented zero-change result. The dashboard window ends at its disclosed latest recorded date; a historical analysis ends at its selected/as-of date.
- **Timing and dates:** duration validity and historical preservation; existing leap/month/year, local-calendar, rest-deadline and catch-up regressions. No claim that a PWA can behave as a native background alarm.
- **Persistence:** existing regression tests for canonical revisions, read-only reports, unit-stamped snapshots, sync conflicts, Unicode archive round trips and preserved raw input. No SQL migration or new collection of other users' data.

Six of the initial 13 new oracle test groups failed before correction. Follow-up tests cover additional code-path findings, including backdated mass, protected trends, the failed-set anchor, unit boundary and save/UI wiring. The final file has 19 groups, with parameterized numerical cases inside several groups. A passing assertion count must not be presented as a probability of injury prevention or prediction accuracy.

## Research checked and implications

- [Nuzzo et al., Sports Medicine 2024 (online 2023)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40279-023-01937-7): 269 studies, 952 tests, 7,289 people. Explicitly models between-person variation and distinguishes bench/leg press. The authors note their search was thorough, not necessarily systematic/exhaustive. Supports uncertainty rather than universal rep-to-weight precision. Publisher article/methods inspected.
- [Halperin et al., 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34542869/): 12 studies/414 participants in the meta-analysis, imperfect RIR prediction with large heterogeneity. Supports cautious interpretation; no fixed personal correction is inferred. Indexed abstract checked; no new claim of full-text access.
- [Ruiz-Alias et al., online 2024 / issue 2025](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11556642/): 26 recreational lifters, bench press with different equipment/relative loads. Indexed study findings checked. Bench-only results do not validate all exercises, machines, or a sex-specific first-load multiplier.
- [Qin et al., 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13102-025-01098-2): compares generalized proximity-to-failure methods in Smith bench press. The app has no bar-velocity sensor; no velocity-based model is introduced. Publisher source inspected.
- [Marzagao, March 2026 preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.17495): large real-world dataset (303,494 near-failure sets), but no directly measured maxima; evaluates internal consistency. The abstract does NOT validate true 1RM accuracy, cross-exercise first-use safety, or a model for RepArc. Not adopted as a production replacement merely because its dataset is large.
- Existing [NSCA/CSCCa transition guidance](https://www.nsca.com/about-us/position-statements/safe-return-to-training/) supports gradual return. It does not validate RepArc's exact reductions. Detailed source boundaries remain in HISTORY-START-EVIDENCE.md and FINAL-SAFETY-AUDIT-12.0.3.md.

## Residual limitations

Self-reported RIR, pain/readiness, load-entry conventions, equipment mechanics, technique and missing recovery limit individual prediction. Two physical machines with the same name can differ. Existing questionable entries cannot be automatically declared correct. Old overwritten current bodyweight and unrecorded historical information cannot be reconstructed honestly. The descriptive Epley-based score and provisional user-reviewed Phase 2 starting estimates are not measured maxima. Keep the existing per-exercise calibration and return safeguards; no claim of zero errors, guaranteed safety, universal exercise optimality or a known accuracy percentage is justified. A qualified independent program review and prospective outcome validation remain separate work.

New recommendations use `12.2.1-policy6`; detailed reports use `12.2.1-review3`. Previous raw sets/recommendation snapshots are not rewritten. Derived completion, adherence, volume and trend displays can correctly change after this release.
